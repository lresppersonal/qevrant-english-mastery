[A] Meat and dairy are central to many diets, and demand for them tends to rise as countries become wealthier. Yet livestock farming uses a large share of the world's agricultural land and is responsible for a significant proportion of greenhouse gas emissions, especially methane from cattle. As the global population grows, scientists, companies and governments are looking for alternatives.

[B] The most familiar option is plant-based protein. Beans, lentils, chickpeas and soya have fed people for thousands of years and require far less land and water than beef. In recent years, food companies have used them to create products designed to imitate the taste and texture of meat. Sales grew quickly at first, but in several markets they have since levelled off, partly because of price and partly because some consumers are wary of heavily processed ingredients.

[C] A more radical approach is cultivated meat, grown from animal cells in large steel tanks rather than taken from slaughtered animals. Its supporters argue that it could provide real meat with a fraction of the land use and without animal suffering. Major challenges remain, however, including the cost of production at scale and the energy required to run the facilities. Only a few countries have so far approved its sale.

[D] Insects are another candidate. In many parts of the world, crickets, grasshoppers and larvae are already eaten as ordinary food, and they convert feed into protein very efficiently. In countries where insects are not traditionally eaten, the main obstacle is cultural rather than nutritional: many people simply find the idea unpleasant. Ground into flour and added to pasta or snacks, insects may become more acceptable.

[E] Most experts agree that no single solution will replace conventional meat. A more realistic future may combine several: eating smaller amounts of higher-quality meat, more traditional plant foods, and new products where they prove affordable and safe. As with many environmental questions, the challenge is as much about habits and culture as about technology.
