[A] Fifty years ago, many city planners thought the bicycle belonged to the past. Roads were built for cars, and cycling was seen as slow, dangerous or only for children. Today, in many cities around the world, the picture looks very different. In the morning rush hour, there can be long lines of cyclists waiting at traffic lights.

[B] Part of the reason is practical. In a crowded city centre, a bicycle is often the fastest way to travel short distances. You do not need to look for a parking space, and you do not need to wait for a bus. For journeys of a few kilometres, cyclists often arrive before drivers.

[C] Cities have also changed their streets. Some have built separate cycle lanes, protected from cars by a kerb or a line of plants. These lanes make a big difference: many people say they would like to cycle but feel unsafe sharing the road with heavy traffic. When the lanes appear, new cyclists often appear too.

[D] Shared bikes are another reason. With a phone app, you can unlock a bike in one part of the city and leave it in another. Electric bikes make hills and longer trips easier, which means cycling is no longer only for the young and fit.

[E] Not everyone is happy. Some shop owners worry that fewer parking spaces will mean fewer customers, although studies in several cities suggest that people who walk and cycle often visit shops more frequently. Drivers sometimes complain that new lanes make traffic worse, especially while they are being built.

[F] Perhaps the most important change is in how people think about streets. A street is not only a road for moving vehicles; it is also a place where people meet, shop and play. More bicycles can mean quieter, cleaner and friendlier streets — but only if cities plan them carefully and listen to everyone who uses them.
