## overview | overview | Definition and function
A verb pattern is the structure that follows a verb. Patterns are largely fixed, so they must be learned with the verb: *avoid **doing***, *decide **to do***, *make someone **do***.

## form | form | The main patterns
| Pattern | Common verbs | Example |
| verb + -ing | avoid, enjoy, mind, consider, deny, risk, suggest, can't help, admit, postpone | *She **avoided answering**.* |
| verb + to-inf | decide, hope, refuse, manage, afford, agree, promise, seem, tend, fail | *They **refused to pay**.* |
| verb + object + to-inf | ask, tell, want, allow, encourage, persuade, expect, force | *I **persuaded him to stay**.* |
| verb + object + bare inf | make, let, help (also with to) | *They **made us wait**.* |
| verb + that-clause | suggest, insist, recommend, admit, claim | *She **suggested that we leave**.* |
| preposition + -ing | interested in, insist on, look forward to, object to | *I **look forward to hearing** from you.* |
!warning After a preposition, always use -ing. *to* is a preposition in *look forward to, object to, be used to, when it comes to*: ~~look forward to hear~~.

## meaning | contrasts | Verbs that change meaning
| Verb | + -ing | + to-infinitive |
| remember / forget | memory of an earlier action: *I remember **locking** the door.* | not forgetting a duty: *Remember **to lock** the door.* |
| stop | end an activity: *He stopped **smoking**.* | stop in order to: *He stopped **to smoke**.* |
| try | experiment: *Try **restarting** it.* | make an effort: *I tried **to open** it but couldn't.* |
| regret | sorry about the past: *I regret **saying** that.* | formal bad news: *We regret **to inform** you…* |
| mean | involve: *A new job means **moving**.* | intend: *I didn't mean **to upset** you.* |
| go on | continue the same: *She went on **talking**.* | move to a new thing: *She went on **to become** a judge.* |

## perception | uses | Verbs of perception
*see, hear, watch, notice, feel* + object + **bare infinitive** (complete action) or **-ing** (action in progress):
> ex: I **saw** him **cross** the road. || I saw the whole action
> ex: I **saw** him **crossing** the road. || in the middle of the action
In the passive, *make* and perception verbs take *to*: *He was **made to** apologise.* *She was **seen to** leave.*

## advanced | uses | Advanced usage and stylistic nuances
- Perfect and passive forms: *He denies **having taken** the money.* *She hates **being interrupted**.* *I'd like **to have been** there.*
- Possessive + -ing (formal): *I object to **his** smoking indoors* (informal: *him smoking*).
- *It* as preparatory object: *I find **it** difficult **to concentrate**. I'd appreciate **it** if you could reply.*
- *suggest / recommend / insist* + **that** + (should) + base form (formal): *She insisted that he **be** present.*

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: He suggested me to apply. || *He suggested (that) I apply / suggested applying.*
> x: I'm looking forward to meet you. || *to meeting you*.
> x: They let us to leave early. || *let* + bare infinitive: *let us leave*.
> x: I avoid to eat sugar. || *avoid eating*.

## register | register | Formal and academic use
Formal writing prefers nominal or that-clause patterns: *The committee recommended **that the policy be reviewed***. Verbs such as *tend to, appear to, fail to* are frequent in academic hedging: *Earlier studies **tended to overlook** this group.*

## summary | summary | Summary
- Learn each verb with its pattern; prepositions take -ing.
- *remember, forget, stop, try, regret, mean, go on* change meaning.
- *make/let* + bare infinitive (active), *be made to* (passive).
- Perception verbs: bare infinitive (whole action) vs -ing (in progress).

=== practice
### [mc] @form B2
Would you mind ___ the window? It's freezing.
+ closing
- to close
- close
- that you close
> *mind* + -ing.

### [gap] @form B2
We can't afford ___ (buy) a new car this year.
= to buy
> *afford* + to-infinitive.

### [mc] @form B2
My parents never let me ___ out after ten.
+ go
- to go
- going
- went
> *let* + object + bare infinitive.

### [error] @errors B2
Which part is incorrect? *The doctor suggested me to take more exercise and sleep longer.*
+ suggested me to take
- The doctor
- more exercise
- sleep longer
> *suggest* is not used with object + to-inf: *suggested that I take / suggested taking*.

### [mc] @meaning B2+
I clearly remember ___ the oven off, so don't worry.
+ turning
- to turn
- turn
- to have turn
> Memory of an earlier action → *remember* + -ing.

### [mc] @meaning B2+
The car broke down, so we stopped ___ for help.
+ to ask
- asking
- ask
- for asking
> *stop to do* = stop in order to do something.

### [gap] @meaning B2+
If the printer doesn't work, try ___ (switch) it off and on again.
= switching
> *try* + -ing = experiment to see what happens.

### [transform] @perception C1
Complete with 3 words (passive). ‘They made the passengers wait for three hours.’ → ‘The passengers ___ for three hours.’
= were made to wait
> In the passive, *make* takes *to*.

### [mc] @advanced C1
The minister denied ___ any money from the company.
+ having received
- to have received
- to receive
- have received
> *deny* + -ing; perfect -ing for an earlier action.

### [context] @advanced C1
‘I would appreciate ___ if you could send the report by Friday.’
= it
> Preparatory object *it* before an if-clause.

### [mc] @meaning C2
After years as a journalist, she went on ___ one of the country's leading novelists.
+ to become
- becoming
- become
- to becoming
> *go on to do* = move on to a new achievement.
