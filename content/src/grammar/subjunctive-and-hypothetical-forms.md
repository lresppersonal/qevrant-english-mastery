## core | core | Core concept
English uses a few special verb forms to show that something is **not a fact**: it is required, recommended, imagined or hypothetical. The most important are the **mandative subjunctive** (*insist that he **be** present*) and the **were-subjunctive** (*if I **were** you*). They are not about time; they mark meaning.
!rule After verbs and adjectives of demand or importance (*insist, recommend, essential, vital*), formal English uses the base form for every person: *that she **attend**, that they **be** informed*.
> ex: The committee recommended that the report **be** published. || a requirement, not a fact (formal)
> ex: He behaves as if he **were** the owner. || imagined: he isn't
In everyday British English, *should* + infinitive (*that she should attend*) or an ordinary tense (*that she attends*) is common. Recognise all three, and use the subjunctive in formal writing.

## overview | overview | Overview and purpose
Compare *The board insisted that the director **resigns*** (British, everyday), *... that the director **should resign*** (British, neutral) and *... that the director **resign*** (formal; standard in American English). The last uses the **mandative subjunctive**: the base form of the verb for every person, with no *-s* and no tense. Alongside it, the **were-subjunctive** (*If I **were** you, as if he **were** in charge*) marks situations as unreal. These forms are few, but they appear in exactly the contexts advanced users need: formal recommendations, legal and academic texts, and careful hypothetical reasoning.

## theory | theory | How it works: marking non-factual meaning
The indicative presents something as fact (*He **is** late*). The subjunctive presents it as **required, desired or imagined** rather than true. Modern English mostly does this with modal verbs (*should, would, might*) and with **backshift** (using a past form for an unreal present: *I wish I **knew***). The subjunctive survives in three places:
1. **Mandative**: after words of demand, proposal and importance: *insist, demand, recommend, propose, suggest, request, require, ask; essential, vital, imperative, crucial; the requirement, the proposal that ...*
2. **Were-subjunctive**: in unreal conditions and comparisons: *if I were, as if it were, I wish she were, were it not for*.
3. **Formulaic**: fixed expressions: *God save the King, be that as it may, come what may, so be it, suffice it to say, far be it from me to ...*
!rule In the mandative subjunctive the verb does not change for person or tense: *They insisted that he **be** present. It is vital that she **not be** told.* (The negative is *not* + base form.)

## form | form | Formation: the main patterns
| Pattern | Example | Alternatives |
| verb of demand + that + base form | *We recommend that each student **submit** a draft.* | *should submit* (BrE), *submits* (informal BrE) |
| adjective of importance + that + base form | *It is essential that the data **be** anonymised.* | *should be*, *are* |
| noun + that + base form | *the requirement that applicants **hold** a degree* | *should hold* |
| if + subject + were | *If the plan **were** to fail, ...* | *was* (informal) |
| as if / as though + past / were | *She talks as if she **were** the boss.* | *was*; present if it may be true: *It looks as if it **'s** going to rain.* |
| suppose / supposing / imagine + past | ***Suppose** you **lost** your job — what would you do?* | present for a real possibility: *Suppose it rains?* |
| but for / if it weren't for + noun | ***But for** your help, we would have failed.* | *If it hadn't been for your help* |
| it's (high/about) time + past | *It's high time we **left**.* | *It's time to leave.* |
| would rather + subject + past | *I'd rather you **didn't** smoke here.* | same subject: *I'd rather not.* |
| were + subject + to (inversion) | ***Were** the rate **to** rise, demand would fall.* | *If the rate were to rise ...* |

## uses | uses | Main uses
#### 1. Formal recommendations and rules
Regulations, minutes, academic recommendations: *The committee proposes that the policy **be** reviewed annually.* The subjunctive presents the action as required rather than describing a fact.
#### 2. Unreal comparisons
*He behaves **as if** nothing **had** happened* (but something did). *It looks **as if** it **'s** going to rain* (it may well: present tense). The tense choice signals whether the comparison is real or imagined.
#### 3. Hypothetical scenarios in argument
*Suppose / Supposing / Imagine / What if* + past introduce thought experiments: ***What if** everyone **refused** to pay?* Useful in discursive essays for testing a position.
#### 4. Counterfactual causes
*But for, if it weren't/hadn't been for, without* identify the factor that made the difference: ***Without** the grant, the research would never have started.*

## examples | examples | Examples in context, explained
> ex: The university requires that every thesis **be** submitted electronically. || mandative subjunctive in a rule
> ex: It is imperative that the patient **not eat** before the operation. || negative subjunctive: *not* + base form
> ex: If I **were** in your position, I'd ask for a second opinion. || were-subjunctive for an unreal present
> ex: She spoke **as though** she **had** read the whole report. || past perfect: unreal comparison about the past
> ex: **Supposing** the funding **were** withdrawn, could the project continue? || hypothetical scenario
> ex: **Were it not for** the volunteers, the festival could not take place. || inverted formal conditional
> ex: **Be that as it may**, the decision stands. || formulaic subjunctive: *even so*

## contrasts | contrasts | Comparison with related structures
| Pair | Difference |
| *I suggest that he go* / *should go* / *goes* | formal subjunctive / neutral BrE / informal BrE. All acceptable in context; the subjunctive is standard in AmE. |
| *I insist that he is honest* | not mandative: here *insist* = strongly claim a fact, so the indicative is correct. Meaning decides the form. |
| *as if it is* / *as if it were* | real possibility vs unreal: *It looks as if it is broken* (it may be) / *He acts as if he were the owner* (he isn't). |
| *if I was* / *if I were* | both used; *were* is preferred in formal writing and fixed in *If I were you*. |
| *It's time to go* / *It's time we went* | neutral vs implies we are already late. |
| *would rather* / *had better* | preference (*I'd rather you stayed*) vs advice/warning (*You'd better stay*). |

## register | register | Register and stylistic nuance
!register The mandative subjunctive is formal in British English and sounds natural in legal, academic and institutional writing. In conversation British speakers prefer *should* or the indicative. American English uses the subjunctive in all registers after *insist, recommend, suggest*.
Formulaic subjunctives (*be that as it may, suffice it to say, come what may*) are stylistically marked: one per text can add elegance; several sound archaic.

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
!! The doctor recommended that he takes more rest. (formal writing) || The doctor recommended that he take more rest. / … that he should take more rest. || After *recommend that*, formal English uses the base form or *should* + infinitive.
!! It is essential that she doesn't be late. || It is essential that she not be late. / … that she should not be late. || The negative subjunctive is *not* + base form, with no *do*.
!! He talks as if he knows everything, but he knows nothing. || He talks as if he knew everything. || When the comparison is clearly unreal, use a past form after *as if*.
!! It's time we leave. || It's time we left. / It's time to leave. || After *it's time* + subject, use the past form for the present.
!! I'd rather you don't tell anyone. || I'd rather you didn't tell anyone. || *Would rather* + another subject takes a past form for the present or future.
!! But for your advice, I would make a mistake. (about a past event) || But for your advice, I would have made a mistake. || A hypothetical past result needs *would have* + past participle.
## advanced | advanced | Advanced applications
#### Inverted hypotheticals
***Were** the government **to** abandon the target, credibility would suffer. **Had** we **known**, we would have acted. **Should** you **require** assistance, please contact us.* These are formal alternatives to *if*-clauses; *should* expresses an unlikely but possible event.
#### Subjunctive after *lest* and *whether*
Literary: *They hid the letters lest anyone **find** them. All citizens, whether rich **or** poor, ...* (older *be he rich or poor*). Recognise these in literature; use sparingly.
#### Hypothetical *would* in concessions
*Much as I **would** like to help, I can't. Even if that **were** true, it **would not** justify the decision.* Combining concession and the unreal makes a careful counter-argument.

## summary | summary | Summary
- Mandative subjunctive: base form after demand/recommend/essential + *that*; negative *not* + base form.
- Were-subjunctive: unreal conditions and comparisons (*if I were, as if he were, were it not for*).
- *As if* + present for possible situations, past/past perfect for unreal ones.
- *Suppose, it's time, would rather* + past for the hypothetical present.
- *But for / without / if it weren't for* identify the decisive factor.
