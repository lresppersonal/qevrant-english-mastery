## overview | overview | Definition and function
In an active sentence the subject does the action; in a passive sentence the subject **receives** it. Use the passive when the agent is unknown, obvious, unimportant, or when you want to keep the focus on a topic already mentioned.
> ex: The bridge **was built** in 1890. || who built it is not important here
> ex: Penicillin was discovered in 1928. It **was later developed** into a mass-produced drug. || keeps the topic as subject

## form | form | Formation
*be* (in the correct tense) + past participle.
| Tense | Passive |
| present simple / continuous | is made / is being made |
| past simple / continuous | was made / was being made |
| present perfect | has been made |
| past perfect | had been made |
| future / modal | will be made / must be made / should have been made |
| infinitive / -ing | to be made / to have been made / being made / having been made |
!tip Add the agent with *by* only when it is new or important: *The novel was written **by a 19-year-old**.*

## reporting | uses | Impersonal passives with reporting verbs
With verbs such as *say, believe, think, report, expect, know, consider, allege*, there are two patterns:
| Pattern | Example |
| It + passive + that-clause | ***It is believed that*** the painting is a fake. |
| Subject + passive + to-infinitive | *The painting **is believed to be** a fake.* |
| … about the past | *The thieves **are thought to have escaped** by boat.* |
| … in progress | *The minister **is said to be considering** resigning.* |

## variations | uses | Passive infinitives, -ing forms and get-passives
> ex: Nobody likes **being criticised** in public. || passive -ing
> ex: The forms need **to be signed** by Friday. || passive infinitive
> ex: She expects **to have been promoted** by then. || perfect passive infinitive
> ex: He **got fired** for being late. || get-passive: informal; often sudden or unwelcome events
!tip *need + -ing* has passive meaning in British English: *The car needs washing* = needs to be washed.

## causative | uses | The causative: have and get something done
Use **have/get + object + past participle** when you arrange for someone else to do something, or when something unpleasant happens to you:
> ex: I'm **having my hair cut** tomorrow. || I pay someone to cut it
> ex: She **got her bag stolen** on the train. || unpleasant experience
Related active patterns: **have someone do** (*I had the mechanic check the brakes*), **get someone to do** (*I got my brother to help me*).

## contrasts | contrasts | Differences between similar constructions
> ex: I cut my hair. || I did it myself
> ex: I had my hair cut. || someone did it for me
> ex: The report was written last week. || neutral passive
> ex: The report got written in the end. || informal; suggests difficulty

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: The meeting was happened on Monday. || Intransitive verbs (*happen, arrive, die, occur*) have no passive.
> x: It is said that he to be rich. || Mix of two patterns: *It is said that he is rich* / *He is said to be rich*.
> x: I must repair my car tomorrow at the garage. || If someone else does it: *I must have/get my car repaired.*

## register | register | Formal and academic use
Academic and scientific writing uses the passive to focus on processes and results: *Samples were collected from 40 sites and analysed.* Impersonal passives distance the writer from claims: *It has been argued that…*, *X is widely regarded as…*. Don't overuse it: a mix of active and passive reads more clearly.

## summary | summary | Summary
- Passive = be + past participle; agent with *by* only if useful.
- *It is said that… / X is said to…*; past: *is said to have done*.
- Passive infinitive and -ing forms after verbs and prepositions.
- Causative *have/get something done*; *get* is more informal.

=== practice
### [mc] @form B2
The new hospital ___ next year.
+ will be opened
- will open by
- is opening by
- will have opened
> Future passive: *will be* + past participle.

### [gap] @form B2
Our flat ___ (redecorate) at the moment, so we're staying with friends.
= is being redecorated
> Present continuous passive: *is being* + V3.

### [error] @errors B2
Which part is incorrect? *The accident was happened at around midnight on the ring road.*
+ was happened
- at around midnight
- on the ring road
- The accident
> *happen* is intransitive and has no passive: *The accident happened…*

### [transform] @reporting B2+
Complete with 3 words. ‘People believe that the ship sank in a storm.’ → ‘The ship is ___ in a storm.’
= believed to have sunk
> Subject + passive reporting verb + perfect infinitive for a past event.

### [transform] @reporting C1
Complete with 3–4 words. ‘Reports say the president is considering a new cabinet.’ → ‘The president is ___ a new cabinet.’
= reported to be considering | said to be considering
> Ongoing action → *to be* + -ing.

### [mc] @causative B2+
I didn't fix the roof myself. I ___.
+ had it fixed
- fixed it
- had fixed it
- got to fix it
> Causative: *have* + object + past participle.

### [gap] @variations B2+
Most people hate ___ (keep) waiting.
= being kept
> After *hate* → -ing; passive -ing = *being* + V3.

### [mc] @variations C1
The documents need ___ before the deadline.
+ to be signed
- signing by you
- be signed
- to sign
> Passive infinitive after *need* (or *need signing* in BrE).

### [context] @causative C1
‘My uncle ___ his wallet stolen while he was on holiday.’ (unpleasant experience)
= had | got
> *have/get something done* can describe something bad that happened to you.

### [rewrite] @register C1
Make this sentence impersonal for an essay: ‘Many experts argue that tourism harms local culture.’ → ‘It ___ that tourism harms local culture.’
= is argued | is widely argued | has been argued
> Impersonal passive with *It* distances the writer from the claim.

### [mc] @register C2
Which sentence is most appropriate in a methods section?
+ Participants were randomly assigned to two groups.
- We randomly put people into two groups.
- People got randomly assigned to two groups.
- Two groups were randomly assigned participants by us.
> Formal passive without the agent focuses on the procedure; *got* is too informal.
