## overview | overview | Definition and function
Comparison expresses differences or similarities in degree. Advanced users go beyond *bigger than* to say **how much** bigger and to compare with precision.

## form | form | Formation
| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
| one syllable: *cheap* | cheaper | the cheapest |
| ending -y: *easy* | easier | the easiest |
| two+ syllables: *expensive* | more expensive | the most expensive |
| irregular: *good, bad, far* | better, worse, further/farther | the best, the worst, the furthest |
!tip Some two-syllable adjectives take both forms: *simpler / more simple*, *narrower*, *quieter*.

## degree | uses | Modifying comparatives and superlatives
| Big difference | Small difference | With superlatives |
| much, far, a lot, considerably, significantly | slightly, a little, a bit, marginally | by far, easily, quite, the second/third … |
> ex: Rents are **considerably higher** in the capital.
> ex: This is **by far the best** option.
> x: It is very cheaper. || *very* cannot modify comparatives: *much cheaper*.

## structures | uses | Other comparative structures
- **as … as / not as (so) … as**: *It's **not as easy as** it looks.*
- **twice / three times as … as**: *Housing costs **twice as much as** it did.*
- **the + comparative, the + comparative**: ***The more** you practise, **the easier** it gets.*
- **comparative + and + comparative**: *It's getting **harder and harder**.*
- **the same as / similar to / different from**: *My view is **similar to** yours.*

## advanced | advanced | Advanced ways of comparing (C1)
> ex: There's **nowhere near as** much traffic as there used to be. || a big difference (informal-neutral)
> ex: The results were **nothing like as** clear as we hoped.
> ex: Unemployment is **not nearly as** high as predicted.
> ex: Prices are **on a par with** those in Paris. || roughly equal
> ex: **Compared with / In comparison with** 2010, crime has fallen.
> ex: **Whereas / While** the north is industrial, the south relies on tourism. || contrast clause
> ex: Exports rose **by** 5%, **from** 20 **to** 21 billion. || prepositions for change

## contrasts | contrasts | Differences between similar constructions
> ex: further / farther || both for distance; only *further* = additional (*further information*)
> ex: elder / older || *elder* only before nouns for family members (*my elder brother*); not with *than*
> ex: less / fewer || *less* + uncountable; *fewer* + plural in formal English

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: She is more taller than me. || Double comparative: *taller than me*.
> x: It's the most good restaurant. || *the best restaurant*.
> x: My bag is the same like yours. || *the same as yours*.

## register | register | Formal and academic use
Data descriptions combine comparatives with precise modifiers: *Spending rose marginally, whereas investment fell sharply.* Avoid vague intensifiers (*very, really*) in favour of *substantially, slightly, approximately, roughly twice as*.

## summary | summary | Summary
- Use *much/far/considerably* or *slightly/marginally* to show the size of a difference.
- *as … as*, *twice as … as*, *the more…the more*.
- C1: *nowhere near as, on a par with, compared with, whereas*.

=== practice
### [mc] @degree B2
The train is ___ faster than the bus; it takes half the time.
+ much
- very
- too
- more
> *much/far* modify comparatives; *very* does not.

### [gap] @form B2
This is the ___ (bad) film I've ever seen.
= worst
> Irregular superlative.

### [error] @errors B2
Which part is incorrect? *My new flat is more bigger than my old one and closer to work.*
+ more bigger
- my old one
- closer
- to work
> Double comparative → *bigger*.

### [transform] @structures B2+
Complete with 3 words. ‘The more you read, the better your vocabulary becomes.’ → ‘___ read, the better your vocabulary becomes.’ (keep the same structure)
= The more you
> *the + comparative, the + comparative*.

### [transform] @structures B2+
Complete with 4 words. ‘Petrol costs double what it cost in 2015.’ → ‘Petrol costs ___ it did in 2015.’
= twice as much as
> *twice as much as* for multiples.

### [mc] @degree B2+
Tokyo is ___ the largest city in the world by population.
+ by far
- much
- very
- far more
> *by far* modifies superlatives.

### [mc] @advanced C1
The new policy was ___ as effective as the government claimed. (big difference)
+ nowhere near
- not near
- nothing near
- nowhere like
> *nowhere near as … as* = much less … than.

### [context] @advanced C1
‘Salaries in the region are now on a ___ with those in the capital.’
= par
> *on a par with* = at the same level as.

### [mc] @register C1
Choose the best sentence for describing a chart.
+ Sales rose slightly in March, whereas profits fell sharply.
- Sales rose a bit in March, but profits went really down.
- Sales very rose in March, while profits fell very.
- Sales rose slightly in March, whereas profits fell sharp.
> Precise adverbs (*slightly, sharply*) and *whereas* for contrast.

### [mc] @contrasts C2
Please contact us if you require ___ information.
+ further
- farther
- more far
- furthest
> *further* = additional; *farther* is only for distance.
