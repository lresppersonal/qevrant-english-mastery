## overview | overview | Why use them?
**Indirect questions** sound more polite and less direct. **Question tags** are short questions at the end of a statement, used to check facts or invite the listener to agree.
> ex: **Could you tell me where the station is?** || more polite than *Where is the station?*
> ex: You're from Kazan, **aren't you?** || checking information
> ex: It's a lovely day, **isn't it?** || inviting agreement / small talk

## form | form | Formation
#### Indirect questions
!rule After the introductory phrase, use **statement word order** (subject + verb) and no *do/does/did*.
| Direct question | Indirect question |
| Where **is the station**? | Could you tell me where **the station is**? |
| What time **does it open**? | Do you know what time **it opens**? |
| **Did** she **call**? | I wonder **if/whether** she **called**. |
| How much **does it cost**? | I'd like to know how much **it costs**. |
Common introductions: *Could you tell me…? / Do you know…? / I was wondering… / Would you mind telling me…? / Have you any idea…? / I'd like to know…*
!tip Yes/no questions need **if** or **whether**: *Do you know **if** the museum is open?*
#### Question tags
!rule Positive statement → **negative tag**; negative statement → **positive tag**. Use the same auxiliary (or *do/does/did*) and a pronoun.
| Statement | Tag |
| She **is** late, | **isn't she**? |
| They **can** swim, | **can't they**? |
| You **live** here, | **don't you**? |
| He **didn't** call, | **did he**? |
| We**'ve** met before, | **haven't we**? |
+++ Special tags
| Statement | Tag | Note |
| **I'm** right, | **aren't I**? | not ~~amn't I~~ |
| **Let's** go, | **shall we**? | suggestions |
| **Open** the window, | **will you / could you**? | imperatives |
| **Nobody** called, | **did they**? | *nobody, nothing, never* are negative → positive tag |
| **There's** a problem, | **isn't there**? | *there* is repeated |
| **Everyone** agrees, | **don't they**? | *everyone/someone* → *they* |
+++

## uses | uses | Meaning and intonation
- **Falling** intonation on a tag: you are fairly sure and expect agreement. *It's cold, ↘isn't it?*
- **Rising** intonation: a real question; you're not sure. *You're coming, ↗aren't you?*
- **Same-polarity tags** (positive + positive) show interest, surprise or sarcasm: *So you're the new manager, **are you**?*
- Indirect questions are used for politeness, in formal emails and in interviews: *I was wondering whether you could send me the agenda.*
!register *I was wondering if…* and *Would you mind telling me…* are the most polite. *Any idea…?* is informal.

## contrasts | contrasts | Indirect questions vs reported questions
| Indirect question (now, polite) | Reported question (telling what someone asked) |
| Do you know where **she lives**? | He asked me where **she lived**. |
| Can you tell me if **it's** free? | She asked if **it was** free. |
Both use statement word order; reported questions usually also **backshift** the tense.

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: Could you tell me where is the station? || Statement order: *where **the station is***.
> x: Do you know what time does the film start? || No *does*: *what time **the film starts***.
> x: I wonder are they coming. || Add *if/whether*: *I wonder **if** they're coming.*
> x: You haven't seen my keys, haven't you? || Negative statement → positive tag: *have you?*
> x: I'm late, am not I? || Use *aren't I?*

## advanced | advanced | Advanced patterns
> ex: **What** she said I can't remember. || A *wh*-clause as the object, fronted for emphasis.
> ex: It's anyone's guess **whether** the deal will go ahead. || *whether* clause as a subject complement
> ex: **Whether** we like it or not, prices will rise. || *whether … or not* = it doesn't matter if
> ex: I'd be grateful if you could let me know **when** the results will be available. || formal email pattern
!tip In formal writing prefer *whether* to *if*, and always use *whether* after prepositions and before *to*-infinitives: *a debate about **whether** to ban cars* (~~about if~~).

## summary | summary | Summary
!rule Indirect questions: polite opener + (wh-word / if / whether) + **subject + verb**. Tags: opposite polarity, same auxiliary, pronoun subject.
- *I'm…, aren't I? / Let's…, shall we? / Nobody…, did they?*
- Falling tag = expecting agreement; rising tag = real question.

Make polite: "What time does the meeting start?" (Do you know…)
= Do you know what time the meeting starts?
> Remove *does* and use statement order: *the meeting starts*.

### [gap] @form B2
She doesn't eat meat, ___ she?
= does
> Negative statement → positive tag with the same auxiliary.

### [mc] @form B2+
I'm on the list, ___?
- amn't I
+ aren't I
- am I not it
- isn't I
> The standard tag for *I am* is *aren't I?*

### [gap] @form B2+
Let's take a taxi, ___ we?
= shall
> Tag after *Let's* is *shall we?*

### [error] @errors B2
Correct the mistake: "I wonder what does this word mean."
= I wonder what this word means.
> No *does*; statement order *this word means*.

### [mc] @form B2+
Nobody saw the accident, ___?
- didn't they
+ did they
- did nobody
- didn't he
> *Nobody* makes the sentence negative, so the tag is positive; we use *they*.

### [transform] @advanced C1
Write a formal request: "When will the results be available?" (I'd be grateful if you could let me know…)
= I'd be grateful if you could let me know when the results will be available.
> Statement order: *the results will be available*.

### [mc] @advanced C1
There was a long debate about ___ to raise the speed limit.
- if
+ whether
- that
- what
> Use *whether* after a preposition and before a *to*-infinitive.

### [context] @uses B2+
(Seeing someone with a guitar) "So you play in a band, ___?" — showing interest, not a real question.
+ do you
- don't you
- are you
- aren't you
> A same-polarity tag (*play… do you?*) expresses interest or surprise.
