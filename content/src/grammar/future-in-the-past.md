## core | core | Core concept
Sometimes you stand at a point in the past and look **forward** from it. The future-in-the-past forms describe what was planned, expected or destined *at that time*, whether or not it actually happened.
!rule Take an ordinary future form and move it into the past: *will → would*, *am going to → was going to*, *am about to → was about to*, *am to → was to*.
> ex: In 1995 few people imagined that the internet **would** change everything. || an expectation, seen from 1995
> ex: I **was going to** call you, but my phone died. || a plan that was not carried out
The key question is always: *where is the viewpoint?* If it is in the past and you are looking ahead from it, you need one of these forms.

## overview | overview | Overview and purpose
When we narrate or report, we often need to look forward from a point in the past: *In 1990 nobody knew that the internet **would** change everything.* *I **was going to** call you, but my phone died.* *She **was to** become the first woman to lead the institute.* These forms are the past-shifted versions of ordinary future forms. They allow a narrator to show plans, expectations, destinies and **unfulfilled intentions** with precision.

## theory | theory | How it works: a past viewpoint looking forward
Choose a reference point in the past, then take the future form you would have used at that point and **move it one step back**:
| At the time (present viewpoint) | Narrated (past viewpoint) |
| *I'm going to resign.* | *She said she **was going to** resign.* |
| *The train is about to leave.* | *The train **was about to** leave.* |
| *I'll see you tomorrow.* | *He promised he **would** see me the next day.* |
| *I'm meeting the board on Friday.* | *She **was meeting** the board on Friday.* |
| *The report is due to appear in May.* | *The report **was due to** appear in May.* |
| *You are to report at 9.* | *We **were to** report at 9.* |
Whether the future event actually happened is often left open, or clarified by context or by the **perfect infinitive**: *We **were to have** met at noon* = the meeting was planned but did **not** happen.
!rule *was/were going to* and *was/were to have + past participle* frequently imply that a plan was **not carried out**. *Would* and *was to* + base form, in narrative, often imply that the future **did** happen.

## form | form | Formation: the main patterns
| Form | Meaning | Example |
| would + base form | future seen from the past (prediction, promise) | *I knew she **would** win.* |
| was/were going to + base form | past intention or prediction; often unfulfilled | *I **was going to** email you, but I forgot.* |
| was/were about to / on the point of + -ing | immediate future in the past | *I **was about to** leave when the phone rang. We **were on the point of giving** up.* |
| was/were to + base form | destiny (narrative), arrangement or instruction | *It **was to** be his last film. We **were to** meet at six.* |
| was/were to have + past participle | arrangement that did not happen | *The minister **was to have** opened the bridge.* |
| was/were due to | scheduled time in the past | *The flight **was due to** land at 8.* |
| past continuous | arrangement in the past | *I couldn't come — I **was flying** to Rome the next day.* |
| would have + past participle | completed before a future point in the past | *By the end of the year, he hoped, he **would have finished**.* |

## uses | uses | Main uses
#### 1. Reporting predictions and promises
*Everyone thought the reforms **would** fail.* *She promised that she **would** be careful.*
#### 2. Unfulfilled plans and excuses
*I **was going to** finish it last night, but ...* is the standard way to explain a plan that changed. Adding *but* clarifies.
#### 3. Narrative foreshadowing (destiny)
*Was to* and *would* let a narrator reveal the future from a past viewpoint: *Little did he know that this meeting **would** change his life.* *The village **was to** remain isolated for another century.*
#### 4. Interruptions of imminent action
*was about to / was on the point of / was just going to* + *when*: *We **were about to** sign the contract **when** the investor withdrew.*

## examples | examples | Examples in context, explained
> ex: At the time, few economists predicted that the crisis **would** spread so quickly. || prediction from a past viewpoint
> ex: I **was going to** apply for the scholarship, but the deadline had passed. || intention that was not realised
> ex: The building **was to have** opened in 2020, but construction stalled. || planned event that did not happen
> ex: Darwin **was to** spend five years on the voyage, a journey that **would** reshape biology. || narrative destiny
> ex: She **was on the point of** resigning when she was offered a promotion. || imminent action interrupted
> ex: The results **were due to** be published in March. || schedule seen from the past
> ex: He hoped that by spring he **would have saved** enough for the trip. || future perfect in the past

## contrasts | contrasts | Comparison with related structures
| Pair | Difference |
| *was going to* vs *would* | intention/plan (*I was going to tell you*) vs prediction or willingness seen from the past (*I knew you would understand*). |
| *was to do* vs *was to have done* | arrangement (outcome open, or destiny: it happened) vs arrangement that did not happen. |
| *would* (future in the past) vs *would* (habit) | *I knew he would be late* (one future event) vs *Every day he would sit by the window* (past habit). |
| *was about to* vs *was going to* | very soon vs at some point; *about to* is more imminent. |
| past continuous vs *was going to* | fixed arrangement (*I was meeting them at six*) vs intention (*I was going to meet them*). |

## register | register | Register and stylistic nuance
!register *Was to* for destiny and *was to have done* for cancelled arrangements are formal and typical of historical narrative, biography and news. In conversation, *was going to* covers most cases.
Foreshadowing with *would* (*It would prove a costly mistake*) is a hallmark of narrative non-fiction and quality journalism; it creates suspense from hindsight.

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
!! I knew she will pass the exam. || I knew she would pass the exam. || The viewpoint (*I knew*) is in the past, so *will* becomes *would*.
!! I was going to call you, but I have forgotten. || I was going to call you, but I forgot. || The whole sequence is in the past: the plan and its failure both belong there.
!! The concert was to held last week. || The concert was to be held last week. || *Was to* is followed by an infinitive; the passive infinitive is *be held*.
!! We were about leaving when it started raining. || We were about to leave when it started raining. || *Be about* takes *to* + infinitive, not *-ing*.
!! The bridge was to have open in 2020. || The bridge was to have opened in 2020. || *Was to have* + past participle describes a plan that did not happen.
!! He said he is going to come, but he didn't. || He said he was going to come, but he didn't. || After a past reporting verb, *is going to* normally becomes *was going to*, especially when the plan failed.
## advanced | advanced | Advanced applications
#### Foreshadowing for rhetorical effect
*It **was to prove** a turning point.* *This decision **would come back** to haunt the government.* Narrative hindsight adds drama to historical or analytical writing.
#### Hedged reporting of plans
*The agreement **was due to** be signed in June* reports a schedule without asserting it happened; useful when the outcome is uncertain.
#### Combining with negative inversion
*Little **did** they realise that the project **would** take a decade.* *Never **was** the empire **to** regain its former power.*

## summary | summary | Summary
- Move a future form one step back to look forward from a past point: *will → would, am going to → was going to, am to → was to*.
- *Was going to* and *was to have done* often signal plans that did not happen.
- *Would* and *was to* in narrative reveal what happened later (foreshadowing).
- *Was about to / on the point of* + *when* for interrupted imminent actions.
- *Would have done* = completion before a future point seen from the past.
