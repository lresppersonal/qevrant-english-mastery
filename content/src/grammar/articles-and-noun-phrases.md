## overview | overview | Definition and function
A noun phrase is a noun plus everything that describes or limits it: *the **three** **rather old** **wooden** chairs **in the hall***. Determiners (*a, the, some, every, this*) tell the listener whether the thing is known, how much or how many.

## articles | uses | a/an, the and no article: the core rules
| Use | When | Example |
| a / an | one of many, first mention, jobs, classifying | *She's **an** engineer. I saw **a** fox.* |
| the | already known or unique; second mention; superlatives | ***The** fox ran away. **The** sun. **The** best day.* |
| no article | plural or uncountable nouns used in general | ***Dogs** are loyal. **Water** boils at 100°C.* |
> ex: I love **music**. || general → no article
> ex: I love **the music** in this film. || specific → the
> x: The life is beautiful. || General abstract noun → *Life is beautiful.*

## special | uses | Special cases
- **Generic *the*** for a whole class (formal, technical): ***The** tiger is endangered. **The** computer changed work.*
- Institutions with no article when used for their purpose: *go to **school**, be in **hospital**, go to **prison**, at **university*** (but *visit **the** hospital*).
- Places: no article for most countries, cities, lakes, single mountains; *the* for rivers, seas, mountain ranges, plural or "state/kingdom/republic" names: ***the** Volga, **the** Alps, **the** Netherlands, **the** UK*.
- Meals, sports, languages, academic subjects: no article (*have **lunch**, play **chess**, study **law***).

## countability | form | Countable and uncountable nouns
Uncountable nouns have no plural and take singular verbs: *advice, information, research, evidence, equipment, furniture, news, progress, knowledge, accommodation (BrE), luggage, homework, traffic*.
> x: She gave me many advices. || *She gave me a lot of advice / several pieces of advice.*
> ex: The news **is** good. || singular verb
Some nouns are countable and uncountable with different meanings: *a paper* (newspaper/essay) vs *paper*; *an experience* (event) vs *experience* (skill); *a light* vs *light*.

## quantifiers | uses | Quantifiers
| With countable plural | With uncountable | With both |
| many, (a) few, several, a number of | much, (a) little, a great deal of, an amount of | some, any, a lot of, plenty of, most, all, no |
> ex: **Few** people came. || not many (negative)
> ex: **A few** people came. || some (positive)
> ex: **Each / Every** student has a laptop. || singular noun and verb
> ex: **Neither** answer is correct. **None of** the answers is/are correct.

## complex | uses | Complex noun phrases (C1)
Advanced writing packs information into noun phrases instead of using several clauses:
> ex: Prices rose rapidly, and this worried consumers. → ***The rapid rise in prices** worried consumers.*
Tools: noun modifiers (*a **government** report*), compound adjectives (*a **two-hour** delay*, *a **well-known** author*), prepositional phrases (*the impact **of social media on teenagers***), and post-modifying clauses (*a decision **likely to anger voters***).
!warning Compound adjectives with numbers have no plural: *a **five-year** plan*, not ~~five-years plan~~.

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: I am student. || Jobs and roles need *a/an*: *I am a student.*
> x: The most of people agree. || *Most people agree.* / *Most of the people I asked agree.*
> x: We need more informations. || *more information*.
> x: He is in the hospital since Monday. || BrE: *He has been in hospital since Monday.*

## register | register | Formal and academic use
Academic writing relies on long noun phrases and generic reference: *The introduction of tuition fees has led to a decline in applications from low-income students.* Countable/uncountable precision matters: *a growing body of **research**, a number of **studies**, an amount of **evidence**.*

## summary | summary | Summary
- a/an = one of many or first mention; the = known/unique; zero = general plural/uncountable.
- Learn the common uncountable nouns; use *a piece of / an item of* to count them.
- few (negative) vs a few (positive); much/little with uncountable.
- Advanced writing compresses clauses into noun phrases.

=== practice
### [mc] @articles B2
___ is the most popular sport in Brazil.
+ Football
- The football
- A football
- The Football
> General reference to a sport → no article.

### [mc] @articles B2
I read ___ interesting article yesterday. ___ article was about sleep.
+ an / The
- the / An
- a / The
- an / An
> First mention *an*, second mention *the*.

### [error] @errors B2
Which part is incorrect? *The teacher gave us some useful advices before the exam.*
+ useful advices
- The teacher
- gave us
- before the exam
> *advice* is uncountable: *some useful advice*.

### [mc] @quantifiers B2
There were ___ people at the concert, so it felt rather empty.
+ few
- a few
- little
- a little
> *few* = not many (negative meaning); *people* is countable.

### [gap] @special B2+
My grandmother has been in ___ for a week. (as a patient) (write the article or – for none)
= – | - | no article | none | nothing
> Institution used for its purpose → no article (*in hospital*, BrE).

### [mc] @special B2+
Which is correct?
+ the Netherlands, the Danube, Lake Baikal
- Netherlands, Danube, the Lake Baikal
- the Netherlands, Danube, the Lake Baikal
- Netherlands, the Danube, Lake Baikal
> Plural country names and rivers take *the*; lakes usually do not.

### [mc] @countability B2+
Choose the correct sentence.
+ There is a growing body of evidence that diet affects mood.
- There are a growing number of evidences that diet affects mood.
- There is a growing number of evidence that diet affects mood.
- There are many evidences that diet affects mood.
> *evidence* is uncountable; *a body of evidence* is a natural academic phrase.

### [transform] @complex C1
Complete with 4–5 words. ‘House prices rose sharply, which surprised analysts.’ → ‘___ surprised analysts.’
= The sharp rise in house prices
> Nominalisation turns a clause into a noun phrase.

### [gap] @complex C1
The flight was delayed for two hours. → It was a ___ delay.
= two-hour
> Compound adjective with a number: singular noun, hyphenated.

### [mc] @special C1
___ cheetah is the fastest land animal.
+ The
- A
- –
- Some
> Generic *the* + singular noun for a whole species (formal, encyclopaedic).

### [context] @register C2
‘A significant ___ of research has examined the long-term effects of remote learning.’
= body | amount
> Uncountable *research* takes *a body of / an amount of*, not *a number of*.
