## core | core | Core concept
English has no single future tense. Instead, you choose a form that shows **how you see** the future event: as a prediction, a decision, a plan, a fixed arrangement or a timetable. The choice depends on your attitude and your evidence, not on how far away the event is.
!rule *will* = prediction or decision made now · *going to* = plan, or prediction from present evidence · present continuous = arrangement · present simple = timetable.
> ex: Look at those clouds: it**'s going to rain**. || evidence you can see now
> ex: I think it**'ll rain** later. || a prediction based on opinion
> ex: I**'m meeting** the director at ten. || an arrangement in the diary
> ex: The train **leaves** at 8.15. || a timetable

## overview | overview | Definition and function
Future meaning is expressed with modal verbs (*will, might*), present forms (*I'm meeting*, *the train leaves*) and semi-modals (*be going to, be about to, be due to*). Each adds a different shade of certainty or planning.

## form | form | Formation and main forms
| Form | Use | Example |
| will + infinitive | predictions, decisions made now, promises | *I think it **will** rain.* *I**'ll** help you.* |
| be going to | intentions; predictions from present evidence | *Look at the sky, it**'s going to** rain.* |
| present continuous | fixed personal arrangements | *I**'m meeting** Ana at six.* |
| present simple | timetables and schedules | *The train **leaves** at 9.15.* |
| will be + -ing | an action in progress at a future time; polite enquiries | *This time tomorrow I**'ll be flying** to Seoul.* |
| will have + V3 | completed before a future time | *By June we**'ll have finished**.* |

## probability | uses | Talking about probability
Use adverbs and phrases to show how likely something is:
| Certainty | Expression |
| very likely | *will definitely / is bound to / is sure to* |
| likely | *will probably / is likely to / I expect* |
| possible | *may / might / could / there's a chance that* |
| unlikely | *probably won't / is unlikely to / I doubt whether* |
!warning Word order: *probably* comes after *will* but before *won't*: *She'll probably come* / *She probably won't come.*

## time-clauses | uses | Time clauses
After **when, as soon as, before, after, until, once, by the time** referring to the future, use a **present** tense, not *will*:
> ex: I'll call you **when** I **get** home.
> x: I'll call you when I will get home.
> ex: Don't leave **until** you**'ve signed** the form. || present perfect stresses completion
The same rule applies to **if, unless, provided (that), as long as**.

## time | time | Time expressions with future forms
!rule Time expressions do not choose the future form for you — your **reason** (plan, arrangement, prediction, decision) does. But some expressions strongly prefer particular forms.
| Expression | Typical form | Example |
| **by** (Friday / the time …) | future perfect | **By Friday** I'**ll have finished** the draft. |
| **this time next week / at 10 tomorrow** | future continuous | **This time next week** we'**ll be lying** on a beach. |
| **tonight, on Monday** (fixed arrangement) | present continuous | I'**m meeting** Sam **on Monday**. |
| **at 6.15, on 3 May** (timetable) | present simple | The train **leaves at 6.15**. |
| **soon, one day, in the future** | will / be going to | **One day** robots **will do** most of this. |
| **when, as soon as, until, before, after** | present tense in the time clause | I'll call **when** I **arrive** (~~will arrive~~). |
+++ in, within, by, until
- *in two weeks* = at the end of that period: *I'll be back in two weeks.*
- *within two weeks* = at some point before the end.
- *by Friday* = no later than Friday (deadline).
- *until Friday* = continuously up to Friday: *I'll be here until Friday.*
+++

## contrasts | contrasts | Differences between similar constructions
> ex: I**'ll** see Tom tomorrow. || prediction or spontaneous plan
> ex: I**'m going to** see Tom tomorrow. || intention
> ex: I**'m seeing** Tom tomorrow. || arrangement (we've agreed a time)
> ex: **Will you be using** the car tonight? || neutral, polite question about plans
> ex: **Will you use** the car tonight? || can sound like a request or demand

## advanced | advanced | Advanced usage: be + to, future in the past
- **be about to** (very near future), **be on the point of / on the verge of** + -ing.
- **be due to** for scheduled events: *The report is due to be published on Monday.*
- **be to** for formal arrangements and instructions: *The president **is to** visit Paris next week. You **are to** report to reception.*
- **Future in the past**: *I knew she **would** win. We **were going to** move, but…*
- **will** for assumptions about the present: *That**'ll** be the postman.* (See *Uses of will and would*.)

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
!! I will meet my dentist at four tomorrow. || I'm seeing my dentist at four tomorrow. || A fixed arrangement with another person is normally expressed with the present continuous.
!! When I will finish university, I'll travel. || When I finish university, I'll travel. || After time conjunctions (*when, as soon as, until, before, after*) use a present form, not *will*.
!! She will probably not come. || She probably won't come. || In natural English *probably* goes before *won't* (but after *will*: *She'll probably come*).
!! Wait, I'm going to help you with that bag! || Wait, I'll help you with that bag! || A decision or offer made at the moment of speaking takes *will*, not *going to*.
!! Do you come to the party on Saturday? || Are you coming to the party on Saturday? || Asking about someone's arrangement needs the present continuous; the present simple is for timetables and routines.
## register | register | Formal and academic use
Academic writing prefers impersonal forms of prediction: *It is likely that…*, *X is expected to…*, *X is projected to rise by 3%*. Formal notices use *be to* and *be due to*: *The building is due to reopen in spring.*

## summary | summary | Summary
- will = prediction/decision now; going to = intention/evidence; present continuous = arrangement; present simple = timetable.
- Future continuous = in progress at a future time; future perfect = completed before it.
- No *will* after time and condition conjunctions.
