## core | core | Core concept
Many verbs, adjectives and nouns are followed by one particular preposition: *depend **on***, *interested **in***, *a reason **for***. The preposition rarely follows a logical rule and often differs from Russian, so it is best learned as part of the word.
!rule Learn the chunk, not the word: *rely on, aware of, responsible for, an increase in, an impact on*. After the preposition comes a noun, a pronoun or an *-ing* form.
> ex: The outcome **depends on** the weather. || verb + preposition
> ex: She's **responsible for** training new staff. || adjective + preposition + *-ing*
> ex: There has been an **increase in** demand. || noun + preposition

## overview | overview | What is a dependent preposition?
A **dependent preposition** is the preposition that a particular word "chooses". You learn *depend **on***, *interested **in***, *a reason **for*** as single units.
> ex: It **depends on** the weather.
> ex: She's **interested in** urban design.
> ex: What's the **reason for** the delay?
!rule After a preposition, use a **noun**, a **pronoun** or an **-ing** form: *I'm thinking **of moving**.* (~~of move~~, ~~of to move~~)

## form | form | Patterns
| Pattern | Example |
| verb + preposition + noun | She **apologised for** the mistake. |
| verb + object + preposition + noun | They **accused him of** fraud. |
| adjective + preposition + noun/-ing | I'm **good at** organising events. |
| noun + preposition + noun | There's been an **increase in** prices. |
| preposition + -ing | He left **without saying** goodbye. |
+++ Questions and relative clauses: the preposition moves to the end
> ex: What are you **worried about**? || informal question
> ex: the project **(that) I'm responsible for** || informal relative clause
> ex: the project **for which** I am responsible || formal: preposition before *which/whom*
+++

## uses | uses | High-frequency groups
#### Verbs
| Preposition | Verbs |
| **on** | depend, rely, insist, concentrate, comment, focus, spend (money) |
| **in** | believe, specialise, succeed, invest, participate, result (= cause) |
| **for** | apologise, apply, pay, search, wait, blame sb, thank sb |
| **of** | accuse sb, approve, consist, remind sb, warn sb, dream (= imagine) |
| **from** | benefit, recover, suffer, prevent sb, protect sb, differ |
| **with** | agree, cope, deal, provide sb, cooperate, charge sb |
| **to** | belong, object, react, refer, respond, adapt, contribute |
#### Adjectives
| Preposition | Adjectives |
| **of** | afraid, aware, capable, fond, jealous, proud, tired, typical |
| **about** | anxious, excited, upset, worried, sure, enthusiastic |
| **at** | good, bad, brilliant, hopeless, surprised, amazed |
| **with** | angry, bored, familiar, satisfied, pleased, obsessed |
| **in** | interested, involved, experienced, rich |
| **to** | similar, equal, addicted, kind, polite, opposed |
| **for** | famous, responsible, suitable, eligible, grateful (for sth) |
#### Nouns
| Preposition | Nouns |
| **in** | increase, rise, fall, decrease, change, interest, difference |
| **for** | demand, need, reason, respect, responsibility, cure |
| **to** | solution, answer, access, approach, attitude, threat, alternative |
| **of** | lack, cause, example, knowledge, fear |
| **on** | effect, impact, influence, ban, tax, emphasis |
| **between** | difference, link, relationship, contrast |

## contrasts | contrasts | Same word, different preposition
| Phrase | Meaning |
| **angry with** a person / **angry about** a situation | different objects |
| **agree with** a person or opinion / **agree to** a proposal / **agree on** a decision | accept a view / accept a plan / reach a joint decision |
| **think of** (have an idea, opinion) / **think about** (consider) | *What do you think **of** it?* / *I'm thinking **about** it.* |
| **result in** (cause) / **result from** (be caused by) | *Stress can result **in** illness.* / *Illness can result **from** stress.* |
| **an increase in** prices / **an increase of** 5% | the thing / the amount |
| **responsible for** a task / **responsible to** a person | duty / who you report to |

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
!! It depends of the price. || It depends on the price. || *Depend* always takes *on*.
!! She's married with a doctor. || She's married to a doctor. || *Married to* someone.
!! We discussed about the plan. || We discussed the plan. || *Discuss* takes a direct object (but *a discussion about*).
!! I'm looking forward to see you. || I'm looking forward to seeing you. || *To* is a preposition here, so it is followed by *-ing*.
!! The impact to society is significant. || The impact on society is significant. || *An impact on* something.
!! He entered into the room. || He entered the room. || *Enter* takes a direct object; *enter into* means begin an agreement or a discussion.
## advanced | advanced | Academic collocations
> ex: The findings **shed light on** the causes of poverty.
> ex: The study **draws on** data from twelve countries.
> ex: Access **to** education is a key **determinant of** income.
> ex: **In contrast to** earlier research, this survey found no link **between** age and satisfaction.
!register Formal writing often places the preposition before *which/whom*: *the conditions **under which** they work*, *the colleague **to whom** I reported*.

## summary | summary | Summary
!rule Learn the word **with** its preposition. After any preposition, use a noun or **-ing**.
- *depend on, consist of, insist on, succeed in, apologise for*
- *good at, interested in, aware of, similar to, responsible for*
- *increase in, impact on, solution to, reason for, lack of*

=== practice
### [mc] @uses B2
The price ___ the size of the room.
+ depends on
- depends of
- depends from
- depends at
> *depend on* is fixed.

### [gap] @uses B2
She apologised ___ being late.
= for
> *apologise (to sb) for sth*

### [mc] @uses B2
He's very good ___ explaining complicated ideas.
- in
+ at
- on
- with
> *good at* + noun / -ing

### [error] @errors B2
Correct the mistake: "We discussed about the new timetable."
= We discussed the new timetable.
> *discuss* has no preposition.

### [gap] @uses B2+
There has been a sharp increase ___ the number of cyclists.
= in
> *an increase in* + the thing that rises

### [mc] @contrasts B2+
Most members agreed ___ the proposal to extend opening hours.
- with
+ to
- about
- in
> *agree to* a proposal or plan; *agree with* a person or opinion.

### [error] @errors B2+
Correct the mistake: "Social media has a big impact to teenagers."
= Social media has a big impact on teenagers.
> *impact on*

### [gap] @form B2+
I'm thinking ___ (apply) for a master's degree.
= of applying|about applying
> Preposition + -ing.

### [mc] @contrasts C1
Many health problems result ___ poor diet.
- in
+ from
- of
- with
> *result from* = be caused by; *result in* = cause.

### [transform] @advanced C1
Make more formal: "These are the conditions they work under."
= These are the conditions under which they work.
> Formal style: preposition + *which*.

=== recognise
@title An email from a project manager
+ We are very [[grateful for]] your feedback on the draft. || adjective + *for*
+ The final schedule [[depends on]] the client's approval. || verb + *on*
- I'll send the revised version tomorrow.
+ There has been a sharp [[increase in]] costs since January. || noun + *in*
+ Please let me know if you are [[aware of]] any other risks. || adjective + *of*
- Thank you again for your help.

=== check
### [mc] She's very good ___ explaining complex ideas.
+ at
- in
- on
> *Good at* + noun or *-ing*.
### [mc] The new policy had a huge effect ___ small businesses.
- to
+ on
- for
> *An effect on* something.
### [mc] Which sentence is correct?
- We need to discuss about the budget.
+ We need to discuss the budget.
- We need to discuss on the budget.
> *Discuss* takes a direct object.
### [explain] Why is it *I'm used to working* but *I used to work*?
= In *be used to*, *to* is a preposition, so it is followed by an *-ing* form; in *used to* + infinitive, *to* is part of the infinitive.
> After a preposition, the verb takes the *-ing* form.
