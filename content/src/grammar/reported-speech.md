## overview | overview | Definition and function
Direct speech quotes exact words; reported (indirect) speech gives the content in your own sentence. Reporting changes **tenses, pronouns, time and place words**, and **word order** in questions.
> ex: “I'm moving to Oslo,” she said. → She said (that) she **was** moving to Oslo.

## form | form | Backshift and other changes
| Direct | Reported |
| present simple / continuous | past simple / continuous |
| past simple / present perfect | past perfect |
| will / can / may | would / could / might |
| must | must / had to |
| now, today, tomorrow | then, that day, the next day |
| here, this | there, that |
!tip Backshift is optional when the situation is still true: *She said she **lives** in Oslo* (she still does).

## questions | form | Reported questions, orders and requests
- Yes/no questions: *if / whether* + statement word order: *He asked **if I was** ready.*
- Wh- questions: wh-word + statement order: *She asked **where I lived***, not ~~where did I live~~.
- Orders and requests: *tell / ask* + object + (not) to-infinitive: *She told us **not to touch** anything.*

## verbs | uses | Reporting verbs and their patterns
| Pattern | Verbs |
| verb + to-inf | agree, offer, promise, refuse, threaten, claim |
| verb + object + to-inf | advise, encourage, invite, persuade, remind, urge, warn |
| verb + -ing | admit, deny, recommend, suggest |
| verb + preposition + -ing | apologise for, insist on, object to; accuse sb of, blame sb for, congratulate sb on |
| verb + that-clause | acknowledge, argue, claim, explain, point out, maintain |
> ex: “It was me who broke it.” → He **admitted breaking** it.
> ex: “You should see a doctor.” → She **advised me to see** a doctor.
> ex: “You lied!” → He **accused her of lying**.

## stance | uses | Choosing verbs that show stance (C1)
Reporting verbs carry the writer's attitude:
| Neutral | Writer accepts the claim | Writer is doubtful |
| state, report, note, say | show, demonstrate, establish, point out | claim, allege, assert, maintain |
> ex: The study **claims** that sugar causes hyperactivity. || writer is sceptical
> ex: The study **demonstrates** that sleep aids memory. || writer accepts it
Summarising verbs: *outline, summarise, conclude, sum up, highlight, emphasise, dismiss, question*.

## contrasts | contrasts | Differences between similar constructions
> ex: He **said** (that) he was tired. || no object after *say*
> ex: He **told me** (that) he was tired. || *tell* needs a person object
> ex: She **suggested going** by train. / She **suggested that we go** by train. || never *suggested us to go*

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: She asked me where did I work. || Statement order: *where I worked*.
> x: He said me that he was busy. || *He told me / He said (to me)*.
> x: They accused him for stealing. || *accused him of stealing*.

## register | register | Formal and academic use
Academic writing reports sources with present-tense reporting verbs: *Smith (2019) **argues** that… / As Lee **points out**,…* Choose verbs carefully: *claim* and *assert* can distance you from a source; *show* commits you to it.

## summary | summary | Summary
- Backshift tenses, change pronouns and time words; optional if still true.
- Reported questions use statement word order.
- Learn the pattern of each reporting verb.
- Reporting verbs signal stance: *demonstrate* vs *claim*.

=== practice
### [gap] @form B2
“I'm working from home today.” → She said she ___ from home that day.
= was working
> Present continuous → past continuous.

### [mc] @questions B2
“Where do you live?” → He asked me ___.
+ where I lived
- where did I live
- where I did live
- where do I live
> Reported question: statement word order, backshift.

### [mc] @questions B2
“Don't open the window.” → She told us ___ the window.
+ not to open
- don't open
- to not opening
- not open
> Reported order: *tell* + object + *not to* + infinitive.

### [mc] @verbs B2+
“I'm sorry I was late.” → He ___ late.
+ apologised for being
- apologised to be
- apologised that being
- apologised being
> *apologise for* + -ing.

### [transform] @verbs B2+
Complete with 3 words. ‘“You really should apply,” she told me.’ → ‘She ___ apply.’
= encouraged me to | urged me to | advised me to
> verb + object + to-infinitive.

### [error] @errors B2+
Which part is incorrect? *The manager accused two employees for leaking the report to the press.*
+ for leaking
- The manager accused
- two employees
- to the press
> *accuse somebody of doing*.

### [transform] @verbs C1
Complete with 2–3 words. ‘“It wasn't me who took the money,” he said.’ → ‘He ___ the money.’
= denied taking | denied having taken
> *deny* + -ing (or perfect -ing).

### [mc] @stance C1
The writer doubts the report's conclusions. Which verb fits best? ‘The report ___ that the policy has reduced crime.’
+ claims
- demonstrates
- establishes
- shows
> *claim* signals the writer is not convinced.

### [mc] @register C1
Choose the most appropriate sentence for an essay.
+ As Lopez (2020) points out, remote work blurs professional boundaries.
- Lopez said in 2020 that remote work was blurring professional boundaries, she said.
- Lopez told that remote work blurs professional boundaries.
- Lopez suggested us that remote work blurs professional boundaries.
> Present-tense reporting verb for citing a source; *told/suggested us* are ungrammatical.

### [context] @stance C2
‘Critics ___ the proposal as unrealistic and too expensive.’ (rejected it without serious consideration)
= dismissed
> *dismiss something as* = reject it as not worth considering.
