## overview | overview | What ellipsis and substitution do
**Substitution** replaces a word or phrase with a shorter form; **ellipsis** leaves it out completely because the listener can recover it from context.
> ex: "Is it going to rain?" "I hope **not**." || *not* replaces *it isn't going to rain*.
> ex: I wanted a large coffee but they only had small **ones**. || *ones* replaces *coffees*.
> ex: She said she'd help, and she **did**. || *did* replaces *helped*.
> ex: "Coming tonight?" "Can't, sorry." || Ellipsis in informal speech: *Are you* and *I* are left out.
!rule Only leave out or replace what the listener can recover **unambiguously** from the text or situation.

## form | form | The main substitute forms
| Replaces | Substitute | Example |
| a countable noun | **one / ones** | This chair is broken — take the other **one**. |
| a clause after think, hope, expect, suppose, be afraid, guess | **so / not** | "Will he win?" "I **expect so**." / "I **hope not**." |
| a verb phrase | **do / does / did** (+ **so**, **it**, **that**) | He promised to call, and he **did**. / If you want to leave, **do so** now. |
| a verb phrase after modals and auxiliaries | the auxiliary alone | "Can you swim?" "Yes, I **can**." |
| a verb phrase after to | **to** alone | I didn't want to go, but I had **to**. |
| a whole idea | **that / this / it** | "He's resigned." "**That**'s a surprise." |
#### Negative forms with so/not
| Positive | Negative (more natural) | Negative (formal/emphatic) |
| I think so. | I **don't think so**. | I think not. |
| I hope so. | — | I **hope not**. (never ~~I don't hope so~~) |
| I'm afraid so. | — | I'**m afraid not**. |

## uses | uses | Ellipsis in clauses and conversation
- After **and, but, or** you can leave out a repeated subject or auxiliary: *She opened the letter **and** (she) read it twice.*
- In comparisons: *He earns more than I do / than me.*
- After **wh-** words: *Someone's taken my pen, but I don't know **who**.*
- In replies: *"Where are you going?" "(I'm going to the) Library."*
!tip In informal speech, the start of a sentence is often dropped (*situational ellipsis*): *(Have you) Seen Tom? / (It) Sounds good. / (I'll) See you later.* Don't use this in essays.
+++ Ellipsis with auxiliaries: which one to keep?
Keep the **first** auxiliary from the original verb phrase:
> ex: "Have you been waiting long?" "Yes, I **have**." || not ~~I have been~~ (possible but heavy)
> ex: He said it would be finished, and it **will (be)**.
> ex: "Should it have been sent?" "It **should have (been)**."
+++

## contrasts | contrasts | so vs it, one vs it, do so vs do it
| Compare | Meaning | Example |
| **one** | another of the same type | I lost my umbrella, so I bought a new **one**. |
| **it** | the same thing | I lost my umbrella, but then I found **it**. |
| **do so** | formal, replaces an action | Visitors wishing to leave early may **do so**. |
| **do it / do that** | informal, refers to a specific act | Don't touch the oven — I told you not to **do that**! |
| **so** after *think, hope* | replaces a clause | "Is it true?" "I think **so**." |
| **it** after *know, say* | refers to a stated fact | "He's late." "I **know** (~~so~~)." |
!warning *Know*, *sure* and *certain* do not take *so*: ~~I know so~~ → *I know.* ~~I'm sure so~~ → *I'm sure (of it).*

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: "Is she coming?" "I don't hope so." || *hope* forms the negative with **not**: *I hope not.*
> x: I prefer the red shoes to the black. || Fine in speech, but *ones* is needed when the noun is countable and not clear: *the black **ones***.
> x: I'd like a one with a balcony. || *a/an* + *one* needs an adjective: *a **big** one* — or just *one*.
> x: "Can you help?" "Yes, I can help it." || Use the auxiliary alone: *Yes, I can.*
> x: I didn't want to, but I had. || Keep *to*: *but I had **to**.*

## advanced | advanced | Cohesion in formal writing
In academic writing, substitution prevents repetition and links sentences:
> ex: Many studies have examined online learning. **Those** that included adult learners reported higher drop-out rates. || *those* = the studies
> ex: The government promised to reduce emissions but has not **done so**. || formal *do so*
> ex: Urban areas grew faster than rural **ones**. || *ones* = areas
> ex: Some participants improved; **others** did not. || *others* + ellipsis of the verb
!register *Do so*, *those* and *the former / the latter* are typical of formal writing. *Do it*, *that one* and situational ellipsis are typical of speech.

## summary | summary | Summary
!rule Replace nouns with **one(s)**, clauses with **so/not**, verb phrases with **do / an auxiliary / to**. Leave words out only when they are easy to recover.
- *I hope not / I'm afraid not* — never *don't hope so*.
- *I know / I'm sure* — never *know so*.
- Formal: *do so, those, the former/latter*. Informal: dropped subjects (*Sounds great*).

### [mc] @form B2
"Do you think it'll snow?" "I don't think ___."
- it
+ so
- that
- not
> After *think*, *so* replaces the whole clause (*it'll snow*).

### [mc] @form B2+
"Will the shop be open?" "I'm afraid ___."
- no
- so not
+ not
- don't
> Negative with *be afraid*: *I'm afraid not.*

### [error] @errors C1
Correct the mistake: "Is it going to be expensive?" "I don't hope so."
= I hope not.|I hope not
> *Hope* is not used with *don't … so*; say *I hope not*.

### [gap] @form B2
These glasses are too small. Have you got any bigger ___?
= ones
> *ones* replaces a plural countable noun (*glasses*).

### [mc] @contrasts C1
I lost my phone yesterday but luckily I found ___ in my coat.
+ it
- one
- so
- that one
> *it* refers to the same phone; *one* would mean a different phone.

### [gap] @uses B2+
I didn't want to sign the contract, but in the end I had ___.
= to
> Keep *to* when the verb after it is left out.

### [transform] @advanced C1
Rewrite more formally: "If you want to complain, you can complain online." (use DO SO)
= If you want to complain, you can do so online.|If you wish to complain, you can do so online.
> *Do so* replaces the repeated verb phrase in formal English.

### [mc] @uses C1
"Have you ever been to Peru?" "Yes, I ___."
- did
- went
+ have
- had been
> Keep the first auxiliary from the question: *have*.

### [error] @errors B2+
Correct the mistake: "He's left the company." "Yes, I know so."
= Yes, I know.|I know.|Yes, I know it.
> *Know* doesn't take *so*.

### [context] @advanced C1
Some cities invested heavily in cycling infrastructure; ___ did not, and saw little change.
- another
+ others
- the others one
- ones
> *others* = other cities; the verb phrase is substituted by *did not*.
