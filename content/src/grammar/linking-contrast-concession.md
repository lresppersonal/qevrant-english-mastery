## overview | overview | Definition and function
Linking devices connect clauses and sentences: contrast (*however*), concession (*although*), cause (*because of*), result (*therefore*), addition (*moreover*). Each belongs to a grammatical type, which decides what follows it.

## form | form | Grammatical types of linker
| Type | Followed by | Examples |
| conjunction | a clause, inside one sentence | *although, even though, while, whereas, because, so* |
| preposition | a noun phrase or -ing | *despite, in spite of, because of, due to, owing to* |
| sentence adverb | a new sentence (often after a full stop or semicolon) | *However, Nevertheless, Therefore, Consequently, Moreover* |
> ex: **Although** it was raining, we went out.
> ex: **Despite** the rain / **Despite** it raining, we went out.
> ex: It was raining. **However**, we went out.

## contrast | uses | Contrast and concession
- **while / whereas** contrast two facts: *The north is wet, **whereas** the south is dry.*
- **although / even though / though** concede a point: ***Even though** he was ill, he finished the race.* (*even though* is stronger)
- **despite / in spite of** + noun / -ing / *the fact that*: ***Despite the fact that** prices rose, sales increased.*
- **however / nevertheless / nonetheless / even so** start a new sentence.
- **Adverbials of concession** (C1): *admittedly, granted, all the same, be that as it may*.

## cause | uses | Cause and result
| Cause | Result |
| because, since, as (+ clause) | so, so … that, such … that (+ clause) |
| because of, due to, owing to, as a result of (+ noun) | therefore, consequently, as a result, thus, hence (sentence adverbs) |
> ex: The match was cancelled **due to** heavy rain.
> ex: The rain was heavy. **Consequently**, the match was cancelled.
!warning *Due to* traditionally follows *be*: *The delay was due to fog.* In formal writing, prefer *owing to / because of* at the start of a sentence.

## addition | uses | Addition and condition in argument
*Moreover, Furthermore, In addition, What is more, Besides* add points; *provided that, as long as, unless* add conditions (see *Conditionals*).

## contrasts | contrasts | Differences between similar constructions
> ex: Although it was late, … || clause after *although*
> x: Although of the late hour, … || *Despite the late hour…*
> ex: On the other hand… || introduces the other side of an argument
> ex: On the contrary… || says the previous idea is false: *It wasn't boring. On the contrary, it was fascinating.*

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: Despite of the weather, we went. || *Despite the weather* / *In spite of the weather*.
> x: Although he was tired, but he continued. || Use one linker only: *Although he was tired, he continued.*
> x: The economy grew, however unemployment rose. || *however* starts a new sentence or follows a semicolon: *…grew; however, unemployment rose.*

## advanced | uses | Advanced usage and stylistic nuances
- Fronted concession: ***Much as** I admire the plan, it is unrealistic.* ***Difficult though** it may be, we must act.*
- *Whereas* and *while* can begin the sentence for balanced contrast.
- Overusing *Moreover/Furthermore* at the start of every sentence sounds mechanical; vary with *This also…, A further problem is…*.

## register | register | Formal and academic use
Formal: *nevertheless, consequently, thus, hence, notwithstanding*. Neutral: *however, therefore, although*. Informal: *but, so, anyway, still*. Good essays use a range, placed naturally, and not at the start of every sentence.

## summary | summary | Summary
- Conjunction + clause; preposition + noun/-ing; sentence adverb + new sentence.
- *although/even though* vs *despite/in spite of*.
- *because* + clause vs *because of/due to* + noun.
- *on the other hand* (other side) vs *on the contrary* (rejecting).
