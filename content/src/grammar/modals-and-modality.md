## core | core | Core concept
Modal verbs show the speaker's **attitude** to an action or a statement. They answer two kinds of question. *Is it necessary, allowed or advisable?* That is obligation and permission. *How sure am I that it's true?* That is deduction and probability. The same verb, especially *must*, can do both jobs.
!rule Obligation: *must, have to, should, can, needn't*. Certainty: *must* (I'm sure it is) → *should, will* → *may, might, could* → *can't* (I'm sure it isn't).
> ex: You **must** wear a helmet. || obligation
> ex: She **must** be tired after the flight. || deduction: I'm almost certain
> ex: She **must have** left already. || deduction about the past: *modal* + *have* + past participle

## overview | overview | Definition and function
Modality expresses the speaker's attitude to a statement. Two main areas:
- **Obligation and permission** (deontic): *must, have to, should, can, be allowed to, needn't*.
- **Probability and deduction** (epistemic): *must, can't, might, could, may, should, will*.

## form | form | Formation
Modal verbs (*can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would*) take a bare infinitive, have no *-s* and form questions by inversion. Semi-modals (*have to, need to, be able to, ought to, be supposed to*) behave more like normal verbs.
| Time | Structure | Example |
| present/future | modal + infinitive | *She **must be** tired.* |
| present ongoing | modal + be + -ing | *They **might be waiting** outside.* |
| past | modal + have + V3 | *He **can't have seen** us.* |
| past ongoing | modal + have been + -ing | *You **must have been dreaming**.* |

## obligation | uses | Obligation, prohibition and permission
| Meaning | Forms |
| strong obligation | *must* (speaker's authority), *have to* (external rule) |
| no obligation | *don't have to, needn't, don't need to* |
| prohibition | *mustn't, can't, aren't allowed to, be forbidden to* |
| advice | *should, ought to, had better* |
| permission | *can, may (formal), be allowed to, be permitted to* |
> ex: You **mustn't** smoke here. || prohibited
> ex: You **don't have to** come. || not necessary, but you may
> ex: We **didn't need to** pay. || it wasn't necessary (we probably didn't pay)
> ex: We **needn't have paid**. || we paid, but it wasn't necessary

## deduction | uses | Deduction: present and past
| Certainty | Present | Past |
| sure it is true | *must be* | *must have been* |
| sure it is not true | *can't / couldn't be* | *can't / couldn't have been* |
| possible | *may / might / could be* | *may / might / could have been* |
> ex: She's not answering. She **must be** in a meeting.
> ex: He **can't have taken** the train; there were no trains that night.
!warning The negative of deductive *must* is *can't*, not ~~mustn't~~: *That can't be right.*

## contrasts | contrasts | Differences between similar constructions
> ex: You **should have told** me. || criticism: you didn't tell me
> ex: You **could have told** me. || reproach: it was possible but you didn't
> ex: **Could** you swim when you were five? || general ability
> ex: I **managed to / was able to** escape. || specific achievement in the past (not *could*)
> ex: You **had better** leave now. || strong advice with a warning; refers to now or the future

## advanced | advanced | Advanced usage: modality beyond modal verbs
At C1 you can express the same meanings more precisely with other structures:
- adjectives: *It is **highly unlikely** that…*, *It is **essential** that…*, *X is **bound/liable/apt** to…*
- adverbs: *conceivably, presumably, inevitably, arguably*
- nouns: *There is **every likelihood** that…*, *There's **no question of**…*, *in all probability*
- verbs: *It **remains to be seen** whether…*, *The data **suggest** that…*
> ex: **In all likelihood**, the trial will be postponed.
> ex: It is **imperative** that every passenger **wear** a seat belt. || formal subjunctive after *imperative/essential*

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
!! You mustn't to wear a tie. || You don't have to wear a tie. || Modals are followed by the infinitive without *to*. And *mustn't* means "it is forbidden"; *don't have to* means "it isn't necessary".
!! He must have been at home yesterday, I'm not sure. || He might have been at home yesterday. || *Must have* expresses near-certainty; if you are unsure, use *might have* or *may have*.
!! I could finish the marathon last year. || I managed to finish the marathon last year. || For one specific achievement in the past, use *managed to* or *was able to*, not *could*.
!! She can't to come tomorrow. || She can't come tomorrow. || No *to* after a modal verb.
!! You needn't to worry. || You needn't worry. / You don't need to worry. || Modal *needn't* takes a bare infinitive; *need* as a main verb takes *to*.
## register | register | Formal and academic use
Academic writing uses modality to **hedge** claims: *These findings **may** indicate…*, *This **could** be explained by…*, *It **would seem** that…*. Strong modals (*must, certainly*) are reserved for well-supported claims. Formal rules use *be required to, be prohibited from, be permitted to*.

## summary | summary | Summary
- Obligation: must/have to; no obligation: don't have to/needn't; prohibition: mustn't.
- Deduction: must (sure), can't (sure not), might/could (possible); past = modal + have + V3.
- *needn't have done* = did it unnecessarily; *didn't need to do* = it wasn't necessary.
- At C1, use adjectives, adverbs and nouns of likelihood to state your stance precisely.

### [mc] @obligation B2
In many countries you ___ carry an ID card; it's the law.
+ have to
- mustn't
- needn't
- don't have to
> An external rule → *have to*.

### [mc] @obligation B2
You ___ bring food; lunch is included.
+ don't have to
- mustn't
- can't
- shouldn't to
> No obligation, not prohibition.

### [gap] @deduction B2
The lights are off. They ___ (be) out.
= must be
> Logical certainty in the present → *must be*.

### [gap] @deduction B2+
She ___ (see) the email; she replied within minutes. (certain)
= must have seen
> Certainty about the past → *must have* + V3.

### [mc] @deduction B2+
He ___ written this report: it's full of errors, and he's very careful.
+ can't have
- mustn't have
- couldn't
- needn't have
> Sure something is NOT true in the past → *can't have*.

### [mc] @obligation B2+
We bought a new heater, but we ___. The weather turned warm the next day.
+ needn't have
- didn't need to
- mustn't have
- hadn't to
> We did it, but it was unnecessary → *needn't have (bought)*.

### [error] @errors B2+
Which part is incorrect? *Despite the storm, we could reach the summit before dark.*
+ could reach
- Despite the storm
- the summit
- before dark
> Specific achievement in the past → *managed to reach / were able to reach*.

### [transform] @advanced C1
Complete with 3 words. ‘It is very probable that the trial will be postponed.’ → ‘In ___, the trial will be postponed.’
= all likelihood | all probability
> *In all likelihood/probability* = very probably.

### [mc] @advanced C1
It is essential that every applicant ___ two references.
+ provide
- provides
- will provide
- must provides
> Formal subjunctive after *essential/imperative/vital that*: base form.

### [context] @register C1
Hedge this claim for an essay: ‘These results ___ suggest that diet affects mood.’ (tentative)
= may | might | could | would seem to
> Hedging with a weak modal or *would seem to* makes academic claims appropriately cautious.

### [mc] @contrasts C1
‘You ___ have told me you were vegetarian! I'd have cooked something else.’
+ could
- must
- can't
- had better
> *could have* + V3 as a reproach: it was possible, but you didn't.

### [context] @advanced C2
‘Whether the reforms will work ___ to be seen.’
= remains
> *It remains to be seen whether…* = it is not yet known.

### [explain] Explain the difference between *You mustn't tell her* and *You don't have to tell her*.
= *You mustn't tell her* means it is forbidden: don't tell her. *You don't have to tell her* means it is not necessary, but you can if you want.
> Negative *must* and negative *have to* have different meanings.
