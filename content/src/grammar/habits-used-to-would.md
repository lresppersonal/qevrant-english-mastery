## overview | overview | Definition and function
Habitual forms describe things that happened regularly or were true for a long period. **used to** and **would** look back at past habits; **be/get used to** describes familiarity; **will** and **would** can describe typical (sometimes annoying) behaviour.
> ex: We **used to** live by the sea. Every summer we **would** swim before breakfast.

## form | form | Formation
| Structure | Form | Example |
| used to | used to + infinitive | *I **used to** smoke.* |
| negative / question | didn't use to / Did you use to…? | *Did you **use to** play the piano?* |
| would | would + infinitive | *He **would** sit by the window for hours.* |
| be used to | be used to + noun / -ing | *I**'m used to** getting up early.* |
| get used to | get used to + noun / -ing | *You'll soon **get used to** the noise.* |
!warning In questions and negatives, *use to* has no *-d*: ~~Did you used to~~.

## uses | uses | Main rules: used to or would?
- **used to** works for both **states and actions**: *I used to have long hair. I used to cycle to school.*
- **would** works only for **repeated actions**, not states: ~~I would have long hair~~.
- **would** normally needs a past time already established; it is common in nostalgic storytelling.
- Use the **past simple** for a single past event or when you say how many times: *I went there twice*, not ~~I used to go there twice~~.

## familiarity | uses | be used to and get used to
These are completely different from *used to* + infinitive. They mean *be/become accustomed to* and are followed by a **noun or -ing form**:
> ex: She **isn't used to** driving on the left. || it still feels strange
> ex: It took months to **get used to** the climate. || the process of becoming familiar
> x: I'm used to get up early. || Use -ing: *I'm used to getting up early.*

## annoying | uses | Typical and annoying behaviour: will, would, always + continuous
- **will / would** (stressed in speech) for characteristic behaviour: *He'**ll** sit there for hours without saying a word.*
- With stress, it shows irritation: *She **WOULD** say that!*
- **always / constantly + continuous** for annoying habits: *You're always leaving the lights on!*

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: I am used to live in a big city. || *I'm used to living…* (familiar) or *I used to live…* (past habit).
> x: I would have a dog when I was a child. || State → *I used to have a dog.*
> x: I use to go to the gym. || For present habits use the present simple: *I usually go…*

## register | register | Formal and academic use
In formal writing, prefer *formerly*, *traditionally*, *in the past* or *tended to*: *Families traditionally ate together*; *Earlier studies tended to overlook gender.* *Would* for past habit is more literary and narrative.

## summary | summary | Summary
- used to: past states and habits that are no longer true.
- would: repeated past actions only, in narrative.
- be/get used to + -ing: be/become familiar with.
- will/would/always + continuous: characteristic or annoying behaviour.

=== practice
### [mc] @uses B2
When I was a teenager, I ___ a motorbike.
+ used to have
- would have
- was used to have
- am used to having
> *have* (= own) is a state, so *would* is not possible.

### [mc] @familiarity B2
I've lived in Manchester for years, so I ___ the rain.
+ 'm used to
- used to
- use to
- would
> Familiarity → *be used to* + noun.

### [gap] @familiarity B2
It took me ages to get used to ___ (drive) on the left.
= driving
> *get used to* + -ing.

### [error] @errors B2
Which part is incorrect? *Did you used to walk to school when you were little?*
+ Did you used to
- walk to school
- when you were
- little
> In questions: *Did you use to…?*

### [mc] @uses B2+
Every evening my grandfather ___ the newspaper aloud to us.
+ would read
- was used to read
- has read
- would have read
> Repeated past action in a nostalgic narrative → *would*.

### [mc] @uses B2+
I ___ to Prague four times as a student.
+ went
- used to go
- would go
- was going
> A specific number of times → past simple, not a habit form.

### [transform] @familiarity B2+
Complete with 3–4 words. ‘Working at night still feels strange to me.’ → ‘I'm ___ at night yet.’
= not used to working
> Not yet familiar → *not used to* + -ing.

### [mc] @annoying C1
‘You ___ leaving your wet towels on the floor!’ (irritated)
+ 're always
- always
- would always be
- used to always
> *always* + present continuous expresses irritation at a repeated habit.

### [context] @annoying C1
‘She failed again? Well, she ___ refuse to take advice.’ (typical, annoying behaviour)
= will | would
> Stressed *will/would* for characteristic, often irritating behaviour.

### [rewrite] @register C1
Rewrite more formally: ‘In the past, families used to eat together.’ → ‘Families ___ ate together.’
= traditionally | formerly
> Formal writing prefers adverbs such as *traditionally* or *formerly*.
