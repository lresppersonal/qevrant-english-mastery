## overview | overview | Definition and function
A conditional sentence has a **condition clause** (often with *if*) and a **result clause**. The tenses show whether the situation is real or imagined, and its time.

## form | form | Formation: the main patterns
| Type | Condition | Result | Meaning |
| zero | present simple | present simple | general truths: *If you heat ice, it melts.* |
| first | present simple | will / may / can + inf | real future: *If it rains, we'll stay in.* |
| second | past simple | would / could / might + inf | unreal present/future: *If I had time, I'd help.* |
| third | past perfect | would have + V3 | unreal past: *If we'd left earlier, we'd have caught it.* |
!tip *If I were* is preferred to *If I was* in formal English: *If I were you, I'd apologise.*

## mixed | uses | Mixed conditionals
Mix times when a past condition has a present result, or a present state explains a past result:
> ex: If I **had taken** the job, I **would be living** in Berlin now. || past condition → present result
> ex: If she **weren't** so shy, she **would have spoken** up at the meeting. || present characteristic → past result

## alternatives | uses | Alternatives to if
| Conjunction | Meaning | Example |
| unless | except if | *We'll go **unless** it rains.* |
| provided / providing (that), as long as, on condition that | only if | *You can borrow it **as long as** you return it.* |
| suppose / supposing, what if, imagine | hypothetical | ***Supposing** you lost your job, what would you do?* |
| otherwise, or (else) | if not | *Hurry, **otherwise** we'll be late.* |
| but for, without | if it were not for | ***But for** your help, I would have failed.* |
| in case | as a precaution (not = if) | *Take an umbrella **in case** it rains.* |

## unreal | uses | The unreal past: wish, if only, it's time, would rather
| Structure | Refers to | Example |
| wish / if only + past simple | present situation | *I wish I **knew** the answer.* |
| wish / if only + past perfect | past regret | *If only I **had listened**.* |
| wish + would | annoying behaviour, wanted change | *I wish you **would** stop interrupting.* |
| it's (high) time + past simple | something should already happen | *It's time we **left**.* |
| would rather + past simple | preference about someone else | *I'd rather you **didn't** smoke.* |
!warning You cannot use *wish + would* about yourself: ~~I wish I would be taller~~ → *I wish I were taller*.

## contrasts | contrasts | Differences between similar constructions
> ex: If I see him, I'll tell him. || real possibility
> ex: If I saw him, I'd tell him. || less likely or imaginary
> ex: Take a coat **in case** it gets cold. || precaution, whether or not it gets cold
> ex: Take a coat **if** it gets cold. || only when it is cold
> ex: **Should** you need help, call me. || formal, less likely (inverted first conditional)

## errors | errors | Common mistakes
> x: If I would have known, I would have come. || No *would* in the if-clause: *If I had known…*
> x: Unless you don't hurry, you'll miss the bus. || *unless* is already negative: *Unless you hurry…*
> x: I wish I can speak Japanese. || *I wish I could speak Japanese.*

## advanced | uses | Advanced usage and stylistic nuances
- **Inverted conditionals** (formal): *Had I known* = If I had known; *Were it not for* = If it were not for; *Should you require* = If you require.
- **Implied conditions** without a condition clause: *I'd have called, but my battery died.* / *A smaller company would never have survived.*
- **if + will** for willingness or result: *If you'll follow me, I'll show you your room.*
- **if + should** for unlikely events: *If anything should go wrong, call this number.*

## register | register | Formal and academic use
Academic writers use conditionals to state assumptions and limitations: *If these trends continue, demand will exceed supply by 2040.* *Were the sample larger, the results might differ.* Inversion and *provided that* sound formal; *as long as* and *what if* are neutral or informal.

## summary | summary | Summary
- Zero/first = real; second/third = unreal present/past; mix them for cross-time meaning.
- *unless* = except if; *in case* = precaution; *provided/as long as* = only if.
- Unreal past after *wish, if only, it's time, would rather*.
- Formal inversion: *Had I…, Were it…, Should you…*

=== practice
### [mc] @form B2
If you ___ water to 100°C, it boils.
+ heat
- will heat
- heated
- would heat
> General truth → zero conditional (present + present).

### [gap] @form B2+
If we ___ (leave) ten minutes earlier, we would have caught the train.
= had left | 'd left
> Unreal past → past perfect in the if-clause.

### [mc] @mixed C1
If I had studied medicine, I ___ a doctor now.
+ would be
- would have been
- will be
- had been
> Past condition, present result → mixed conditional.

### [mc] @alternatives B2+
You can borrow my car ___ you fill it up afterwards.
+ as long as
- unless
- in case
- otherwise
> *as long as* = only if (a condition).

### [mc] @contrasts B2+
Take some cash ___ the card machine isn't working.
+ in case
- if
- unless
- provided
> A precaution, not a condition → *in case*.

### [gap] @unreal B2+
I wish I ___ (not / say) that to her yesterday.
= hadn't said | had not said
> Past regret → wish + past perfect.

### [gap] @unreal C1
It's high time the government ___ (do) something about housing.
= did
> *It's (high) time* + past simple for something that should already happen.

### [error] @errors B2+
Which part is incorrect? *Unless you don't book now, there won't be any seats left.*
+ Unless you don't book
- now
- there won't be
- any seats left
> *Unless* already means *if not*: *Unless you book now…*

### [transform] @advanced C1
Complete with 3 words (formal inversion). ‘If I had known about the strike, I would have driven.’ → ‘___ about the strike, I would have driven.’
= Had I known
> Inverted third conditional: *Had* + subject + past participle.

### [transform] @advanced C1
Complete with 4 words. ‘Your support was the only reason we succeeded.’ → ‘___ your support, we would not have succeeded.’
= If it hadn't been for | If it had not been for | Had it not been for | But for
> *If it hadn't been for / But for* + noun = without.

### [mc] @unreal C1
I'd rather you ___ my phone without asking.
+ didn't use
- don't use
- not use
- wouldn't use
> *would rather* + another subject + past simple.

### [context] @advanced C2
‘___ any further information be required, please do not hesitate to contact us.’
= Should
> Inverted first conditional in formal letters: *Should* + subject + infinitive.
