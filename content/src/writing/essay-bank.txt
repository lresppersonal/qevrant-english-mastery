== type: opinion
@title Opinion essays
@recognise "To what extent do you agree or disagree?" / "Do you agree or disagree?" / "Is this a positive or negative idea?"
@structure Introduction: paraphrase the question and state your position clearly; Body 1: your strongest reason, explained and illustrated; Body 2: a second reason, or a concession followed by your counter-argument; Conclusion: restate your position and summarise the reasons
@linking In my view,; I would argue that; It is undeniable that; Admittedly,; Nevertheless,; For this reason,; On balance,
Q education | B2+ | Grades in primary school | Some educators believe that young children should not receive grades at school, only written feedback. To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Position: partly agree; Body 1: grades can create anxiety and comparison at an early age; Body 2: but parents and teachers need clear indicators of progress; Conclusion: feedback first, simple grades later | formative assessment; motivation; self-esteem; benchmark | give feedback; track progress; foster a love of learning | Grades motivate some children but discourage others; Written feedback is more informative but takes more teacher time; Early labels can become self-fulfilling
Q science-technology | C1 | Banning phones in schools | Smartphones should be completely banned from schools during the school day. To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Position: agree with exceptions; Body 1: distraction and cyberbullying undermine learning; Body 2: phones can be useful tools, so controlled use in lessons may be justified; Conclusion | distraction; attention span; digital literacy; enforce | impose a ban; pose a threat to; undermine concentration | Bans improve focus and social interaction; Parents want to contact children in emergencies; Pupils need to learn responsible use
Q environment | C1 | Paying for plastic | Governments should make manufacturers, not consumers, pay for the cost of recycling plastic packaging. Do you agree or disagree? | Position: agree; Body 1: producers decide how products are packaged; Body 2: costs would encourage less packaging; concede prices may rise; Conclusion | producer responsibility; incentive; landfill; biodegradable | bear the cost; reduce waste; shift responsibility | The polluter-pays principle; Companies pass costs on to consumers; Design changes at source are more effective than consumer behaviour
Q health-medicine | B2+ | Taxing sugary drinks | Taxing sugary drinks is the most effective way to reduce obesity. To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Position: disagree that it is the MOST effective; Body 1: taxes can reduce consumption; Body 2: obesity has many causes; education, urban design and food labelling also matter; Conclusion | obesity; sedentary; levy; public health | childhood obesity; healthy eating; change behaviour | Taxes raise revenue for health programmes; Poorer households pay a larger share of income; Exercise and diet education address root causes
Q work-careers | C1 | Remote work and young employees | Working from home is harmful to the careers of young employees. Do you agree or disagree? | Position: largely agree; Body 1: informal learning and mentoring happen in person; Body 2: but flexibility and wider job access can benefit them; Conclusion: hybrid arrangements | mentor; visibility; isolation; productivity | learn on the job; career progression; work-life balance | Young staff learn by observing colleagues; Promotions may favour visible employees; Remote work opens jobs beyond big cities
Q arts-culture | C1 | Public money for the arts | Public money should be spent on hospitals and schools rather than on theatres and museums. To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Position: disagree; Body 1: culture brings social and economic benefits; Body 2: concede priorities in times of crisis; Conclusion | subsidise; cultural heritage; well-being; tourism revenue | allocate funds; public spending; enrich society | Arts budgets are small compared with health; Culture attracts tourists and investment; Access to arts should not depend on wealth

== type: discussion
@title Discussion essays
@recognise "Discuss both these views and give your own opinion."
@structure Introduction: present the two views and signal your opinion; Body 1: the first view, explained fairly; Body 2: the second view, explained fairly; Conclusion: your opinion, with the main reason
@linking Some people argue that; Others contend that; Proponents of; On the other hand,; Critics maintain that; Having considered both views,; I am inclined to believe that
Q education | B2+ | Practical skills or academic subjects | Some people think schools should teach practical skills such as cooking and managing money, while others believe academic subjects are more important. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. | Intro; Body 1: life skills prepare young people for independence; Body 2: academic subjects build knowledge and open university routes; Conclusion: integrate both | life skills; financial literacy; curriculum; well-rounded | prepare students for; acquire skills; broaden the curriculum | Many adults lack basic financial knowledge; Time in the curriculum is limited; Practical skills can be taught through academic subjects
Q crime-law | C1 | Prison or rehabilitation | Some people believe that the main aim of prison should be punishment, while others think it should be rehabilitation. Discuss both views and give your opinion. | Intro; Body 1: punishment as justice and deterrent; Body 2: rehabilitation reduces reoffending; Conclusion | deterrent; rehabilitation; reoffend; offender | serve a sentence; reduce reoffending; reintegrate into society | Victims expect justice; Most prisoners are eventually released; Education in prison lowers repeat crime
Q society-politics | C1 | Who should care for the elderly | Some argue that families should take care of elderly relatives, while others believe this is the responsibility of the state. Discuss both views and give your opinion. | Intro; Body 1: family care, tradition and emotional support; Body 2: state care, professional standards, ageing populations; Conclusion: shared responsibility | ageing population; welfare; caregiver; burden | provide care; place a strain on; social security | Families may lack time or skills; State care requires high taxes; Cultural expectations differ
Q communication-media | C1 | Regulating social media | Some people think governments should strictly regulate social media companies, while others believe this would threaten free speech. Discuss both views and give your opinion. | Intro; Body 1: harmful content, misinformation and child safety; Body 2: censorship risks; Conclusion | misinformation; censorship; regulation; accountability | hold companies accountable; spread misinformation; freedom of expression | Platforms profit from engagement; Governments may abuse power; Independent regulators as a middle way
Q transport-movement | B2+ | Free public transport | Some people think public transport should be free for everyone, while others believe passengers should pay. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. | Intro; Body 1: free transport reduces congestion and helps poorer residents; Body 2: cost to taxpayers and need for investment; Conclusion | congestion; infrastructure; subsidy; commuter | reduce car use; cut emissions; fund investment | Fares fund maintenance; Free travel may cause overcrowding; Cities that tried it had mixed results
Q relationships-personality | B2+ | Choosing a partner | Some people believe that it is best to choose a partner with similar interests and background, while others think differences make a relationship stronger. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. | Intro; Body 1: shared values reduce conflict; Body 2: differences encourage growth; Conclusion | compatibility; values; compromise; rapport | share interests; see eye to eye; broaden horizons | Shared values matter more than hobbies; Differences can cause conflict; Communication is key in both cases

== type: advantages_disadvantages
@title Advantages and disadvantages essays
@recognise "What are the advantages and disadvantages?" / "Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"
@structure Introduction: paraphrase; if asked, state which side outweighs; Body 1: main advantages with examples; Body 2: main disadvantages with examples; Conclusion: summarise and give a clear verdict if required
@linking One major advantage is; A further benefit is; The main drawback is; On the downside,; This is outweighed by; All things considered,
Q education | B2+ | Online degrees | More universities are offering complete degrees online. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages? | Verdict: advantages outweigh for adult learners; Body 1: flexibility, cost, access; Body 2: isolation, lower completion, practical subjects; Conclusion | flexibility; dropout rate; self-discipline; accessible | study at your own pace; widen access; face-to-face interaction | Working adults can study; Employers' perception of online degrees; Labs and clinical training are difficult online
Q economics-globalisation | C1 | Global brands | Large international companies are opening branches in small towns around the world. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? | Verdict; Body 1: jobs, investment, consistent quality; Body 2: local businesses close, profits leave, towns look the same; Conclusion | multinational; homogenisation; local economy; profit | create jobs; drive out competition; boost investment | Local shops cannot compete on price; Global brands train workers; Cultural identity of town centres
Q travel-tourism | B2+ | Space tourism | Private companies now offer short trips into space for wealthy tourists. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this development? | Body 1: funding for space technology, inspiration; Body 2: emissions, inequality, safety; Conclusion | emissions; innovation; elite; commercial | push the boundaries; carbon footprint; luxury market | Revenue drives cheaper launch technology; High emissions per passenger; Symbol of inequality
Q health-medicine | C1 | Health apps | Many people now use apps to monitor their health and fitness. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? | Verdict: advantages if data is protected; Body 1: motivation, early warning; Body 2: privacy, anxiety, inaccurate advice; Conclusion | data privacy; well-being; self-diagnosis; accuracy | monitor your health; set goals; data breach | Tracking encourages exercise; Data may be sold; Obsessive monitoring can cause anxiety
Q cities-urban | C1 | Living in high-rise buildings | In many cities, more people now live in tall apartment buildings. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this? | Body 1: efficient land use, housing supply, services; Body 2: lack of community, green space, fire safety; Conclusion | density; sprawl; amenities; community | make efficient use of land; densely populated; sense of community | Reduces urban sprawl; Families lack outdoor space; Good design matters

== type: problem_solution
@title Problem and solution essays
@recognise "What problems does this cause? What solutions can you suggest?" / "How can this problem be solved?"
@structure Introduction: introduce the situation; Body 1: two specific problems with consequences; Body 2: matching solutions, with who should act; Conclusion: summarise and evaluate
@linking One serious consequence is; This leads to; This could be tackled by; An effective solution would be; Governments should; If these measures were adopted,
Q cities-urban | B2+ | Traffic in city centres | Many city centres suffer from serious traffic congestion. What problems does this cause, and what solutions can you suggest? | Body 1: air pollution, lost time, stress; Body 2: congestion charges, better public transport, cycling; Conclusion | congestion; emissions; commute; pedestrian | traffic jam; congestion charge; public transport network | Health effects of pollution; Delivery vehicles; Park-and-ride schemes
Q environment | C1 | Food waste | A large proportion of food produced worldwide is wasted. What are the problems associated with this, and how can they be addressed? | Body 1: environmental and economic costs; Body 2: supermarkets, labelling, redistribution; Conclusion | food waste; landfill; best-before date; redistribute | throw away; tackle waste; methane emissions | Confusing date labels; Supermarket standards for appearance; Food banks
Q work-careers | C1 | Skills shortages | Many countries face shortages of skilled workers in areas such as healthcare and engineering. Q communication-media | C1 | Online misinformation | False information spreads quickly on the internet. What problems does this cause, and what solutions can you suggest? | Body 1: health risks, political polarisation, loss of trust; Body 2: media literacy, platform design, fact-checking; Conclusion | misinformation; polarisation; media literacy; fact-check | spread false information; erode trust; critical thinking | Vaccine rumours; Teaching students to verify sources; Labels and slower sharing
Q relationships-personality | B2+ | Loneliness among the elderly | Many older people today live alone and feel lonely. == type: two_part
@title Two-part question essays
@recognise Two direct questions, e.g. "Why is this happening? Is it a positive development?"
@structure Introduction: paraphrase and answer both questions briefly; Body 1: answer question 1 with explanation; Body 2: answer question 2 with explanation; Conclusion: summarise both answers
@linking The main reason for this is; This can be explained by; As for whether; In my opinion, this is; To sum up,

== type: positive_negative
@title Positive or negative development essays
@recognise "Is this a positive or negative development?"
@structure Introduction: paraphrase and state whether it is mainly positive or negative; Body 1: the side you agree with less (briefly); Body 2: your main side, developed fully; Conclusion: clear judgement
@linking While it is true that; Admittedly,; However, on balance,; The benefits far outweigh; It is therefore a largely positive/negative development

@linking This is largely due to; stems from; can be attributed to; As a result,; This has led to; Consequently,; One knock-on effect is
